Key numbers at a glance

Four interlinked but distinct realities define migration in Moldova: a large diaspora abroad, a shrinking resident population, refugee hosting since 2022, and an economy underpinned by remittances. Tap any card to explore it on the map.

02

How to read the numbers

Two terms unlock the rest of the dashboard. A stock is a headcount at one moment — how many people are abroad right now. A flow is movement over a period — how many formally registered a move this year. Mixing them is the most common mistake in reading migration data.

Emigration remains Moldova's largest migration reality: the diaspora abroad is roughly six times the refugee population it hosts and eight times its foreign-born residents.

Definitions & common mistakes ▸

Diaspora stock

People born in Moldova now living abroad, counted at one moment. 864,257 (UN DESA 2024). Built up over decades — not a single year's departures.

Foreign-born residents

People living in Moldova who were born elsewhere. 106,700 at the 2024 census (4.4%). A residence stock — most hold Moldovan citizenship.

Refugees hosted

People who fled to Moldova and are protected here. 141,058 Ukrainians (UNHCR, May 2026). A humanitarian count — not the same as foreign-born residents.

Registered flows

People who formally registered a move in a single year — a few thousand (NBS). A flow; tiny next to the stocks, because most who leave never deregister.

Read it right 864,000 Moldova-born people abroad does not mean 864,000 emigrated this year. It is a lifetime stock accumulated over decades.

Read it right 141,000 refugees in Moldova are not the same as the foreign-born residents in the census — different definitions, different sources, different people.

04

What drove the trends, 1991–2026

Moldova's migration story tracks its history: independence opened the borders, crises pushed people out, EU ties pulled them west, and the war next door made the country a major refugee host almost overnight.

05

Labour migration & the diaspora

Most Moldovan emigration is, at root, labour migration: people leaving to work, often sending money home and frequently keeping one foot in both countries. The diaspora's centre of gravity has shifted from the post-Soviet east toward the European Union over the past two decades.

Main destinations · 2024

Bars show Moldova-born residents by destination; the percentage is each country's share of the 864,257 diaspora total. Germany, the US and the UK report residents by citizenship rather than birthplace, so they carry no Moldova-born cell here — their estimated numbers appear in the proxy panel on the Leaving Moldova view.

Trends, return & brain circulation

East → West shift

Russia was the top destination through the 2010s; by 2024 Italy leads and the EU as a whole holds the majority of the recorded diaspora.

Return migration is small

Official repatriation is a thin counter-flow: 332 returnees in 2024, down from 1,462 in 2015 (NBS). Circular and seasonal returns are common but largely unrecorded.

Brain drain vs brain circulation

Brain drain is the permanent loss of skilled and young workers; brain circulation is the more hopeful idea that migrants return with skills, capital and networks. Moldova leans toward drain: about 68% of registered emigrants are under 35.

The diaspora's centre of gravity moved west

Russia was the largest recorded destination through the 2010s; Italy overtook it around 2020 as EU routes opened and post-2014 ties deepened. Moldova-born residents, 2010–2024.

More churn than the register shows

Beyond the small domicile register, NBS publishes a far larger flow series using the international 12-month rule (from border-crossing data). It captures long-term moves the register misses — and emigration has run ahead of immigration every year. Persons per year, 2014–2024.

Data limitations. There is no comprehensive, regularly published public breakdown of the diaspora by occupation, skill level or sector. Qualitatively, Moldovans abroad are concentrated in construction, domestic and care work, agriculture, hospitality and transport, but this dashboard does not chart it for lack of an authoritative, current source. Registered NBS flows also undercount real movement, so labour-migration totals here should be read as lower bounds.

Sources & methodology

Moldova's numbers tell a story that is still unfinished. The diaspora is large, the population is shrinking, the remittances keep the economy afloat, and a quarter-million Ukrainians have passed through in search of safety. What comes next — whether emigrants return, whether the refugee wave recedes, whether remittances outlast the diaspora — will define the country for a generation. The data below is where that story currently stands.
