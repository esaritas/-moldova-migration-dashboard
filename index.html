Moldova in Motion

Roughly one in four people born in Moldova now live somewhere else, yet their money, their families and the crises they pass through still tie them to home.

This is the story of where Moldovans go, who comes to Moldova, and how much money makes its way back. Every bubble is sized by the number of people, or the dollars, travelling along that route.

The last two views come from Moldova's official NBS register, which captures only a few thousand moves a year. That is a far smaller thing than the diaspora and refugee stocks on the left, so read them on their own terms.

Key migration numbers at a glance

Four interlinked but distinct realities: a large diaspora abroad, a shrinking resident population, refugee hosting since 2022, and an economy underpinned by remittances. Tap any card to explore it.

Migration concepts explained (stock vs flow, refugees vs residents)

A stock is a count at one moment (how many people are abroad today). A flow is movement during a period (how many registered a move this year). Stocks are large and slow; flows are small and yearly. Mixing them is the most common mistake in reading migration data.

Diaspora stock

People born in Moldova now living abroad, counted at one moment. 864,257 (UN DESA 2024). Built up over decades — not a single year's departures.

Foreign-born residents

People living in Moldova who were born elsewhere. 106,700 at the 2024 census (4.4%). A residence stock — most hold Moldovan citizenship.

Refugees hosted

People who fled to Moldova and are protected here. 141,058 Ukrainians (UNHCR, May 2026). A humanitarian count — not the same as foreign-born residents.

Registered flows

People who formally registered a move in a single year — a few thousand (NBS). A flow; tiny next to the stocks, because most who leave never deregister.

Read it right 864,000 Moldova-born people abroad does not mean 864,000 emigrated this year. It is a lifetime stock accumulated over decades.

Read it right 141,000 refugees in Moldova are not the same as the foreign-born residents in the census — different definitions, different sources, different people.

Labour migration & the Moldovan diaspora (destinations, return, brain drain)

Most Moldovan emigration is, at root, labour migration: people leaving to work, often sending money home and frequently keeping one foot in both countries. The diaspora's centre of gravity has shifted from the post-Soviet east toward the European Union over the past two decades.

Main destinations · 2024

Moldova-born stock, UN DESA 2024. Germany, the US and the UK report by citizenship, not birthplace, so they carry no Moldova-born cell here and are omitted.

Trends, return & brain circulation

East → West shift

Russia was the top destination through the 2010s; by 2024 Italy leads and the EU as a whole holds the majority of the recorded diaspora.

Return migration is small

Official repatriation is a thin counter-flow: 332 returnees in 2024, down from 1,462 in 2015 (NBS). Circular and seasonal returns are common but largely unrecorded.

Brain drain vs brain circulation

Brain drain is the permanent loss of skilled and young workers; brain circulation is the more hopeful idea that migrants return with skills, capital and networks. Moldova leans toward drain: about 68% of registered emigrants are under 35, concentrating the loss in working and child-bearing ages.

Data limitations. There is no comprehensive, regularly published public breakdown of the diaspora by occupation, skill level or sector. Qualitatively, Moldovans abroad are concentrated in construction, domestic and care work, agriculture, hospitality and transport, but this dashboard does not chart it for lack of an authoritative, current source. Registered NBS flows also undercount real movement, so labour-migration totals here should be read as lower bounds.
How to read this: Stock ≠ flow  ·  Refugee count ≠ immigrant stock  ·  Bank transfers ≠ total remittances  ·  Shown countries ≠ national total
