Key numbers at a glance

Four connected but distinct realities define migration in the Republic of Moldova. There is a large diaspora abroad, a shrinking resident population, the hosting of refugees since 2022, and an economy underpinned by remittances. Tap any card to explore it on the map.

02

How to read the numbers

Two terms unlock the rest of the dashboard. A stock is a headcount at one moment, meaning how many people are abroad right now. A flow is movement over a period, meaning how many people moved during the year. Mixing the two is the most common mistake people make when they read migration data.

Emigration remains the largest migration reality of the Republic of Moldova. The diaspora abroad is roughly six times the refugee population the country hosts and eight times the number of foreign born residents.

Definitions & common mistakes ▸

Diaspora stock

People born in the Republic of Moldova who now live abroad, counted at one moment. The figure is 864,257, from the United Nations in 2024. It has built up over decades and is not a single year's departures.

Foreign-born residents

People living in the Republic of Moldova who were born elsewhere. The figure is 106,700 at the 2024 census, or 4.4 percent of residents. This is a residence stock, and most of them hold Moldovan citizenship.

Refugees hosted

People who fled to the Republic of Moldova and are protected here. The figure is 141,058 Ukrainians, recorded by UNHCR in May 2026. This is a humanitarian count, and it is not the same as the foreign born residents.

Yearly flows

People who move during a single year. In 2024 the National Bureau of Statistics recorded about 105,800 long stay arrivals and 123,400 departures under the international twelve month rule. This is a flow, not a stock, and you can explore it under Labour migration.

Read it right 864,000 people born in the Republic of Moldova and living abroad does not mean that 864,000 emigrated this year. It is a lifetime stock that has accumulated over decades.

Read it right The 141,000 refugees in the Republic of Moldova are not the same as the foreign born residents in the census. They use different definitions and different sources, and they count different people.

05

Labour migration and the diaspora

Most emigration from the Republic of Moldova is, at its root, labour migration. People leave to find work, they often send money home, and many keep one foot in both countries. Over the past two decades the centre of gravity of the diaspora has shifted from the former Soviet east toward the European Union.

Main destinations in 2024

Each bar shows the number of people born in the Republic of Moldova who now live in that country, and the percentage is that country's share of the 864,257 diaspora total. Germany, the United States and the United Kingdom count their residents by citizenship rather than by country of birth, so they carry no figure here. Their estimated numbers appear in the proxy panel on the Leaving Moldova view.

Trends, return and brain circulation

The shift from east to west

Russia was the leading destination through the 2010s. By 2024 Italy leads, and the European Union as a whole holds the majority of the recorded diaspora.

Return migration is small

Official repatriation is a thin counter flow. There were 332 returnees in 2024, down from 1,462 in 2015, according to the National Bureau of Statistics. Circular and seasonal returns are common, but they largely go unrecorded.

Brain drain or brain circulation

Brain drain is the permanent loss of skilled and young workers. Brain circulation is the more hopeful idea that migrants return with skills, capital and networks. The Republic of Moldova leans toward drain, because emigration is heavily concentrated among the young, who leave in their working and child-bearing years.

The centre of gravity of the diaspora moved west

Russia was the largest recorded destination through the 2010s. Italy overtook it around 2020, as routes into the European Union opened and ties deepened after 2014. These figures count people born in the Republic of Moldova, from 2010 to 2024.

The fuller picture of yearly movement

The National Bureau of Statistics publishes a long stay migration series that follows the international twelve month rule, estimated from border crossings. It captures the people who settle abroad or arrive for a year or more, and emigration has run ahead of immigration in every year from 2014 to 2024.

Two different immigration numbers, and why they differ

Flow, per year 105,804 Long-term immigrants The border crossing series, under the twelve month rule, counts anyone who stays for a year or more. About 56 percent of them are Moldovans returning home. This is the chart above.
Stock, at one moment 106,700 Foreign born residents The 2024 census counts the people living in the country who were born abroad, a population built up over decades. This is the Foreign born view of the map.

Why 105,804 and 106,700 look alike but are not the same. A flow is movement during a single year, while a stock is a headcount at one moment. The first number is one year of arrivals, most of them Moldovans coming home. The second is the entire population that was born abroad. The two count different people.

Data limitations. There is no comprehensive, regularly published breakdown of the diaspora by occupation, skill level or sector. In broad terms, Moldovans abroad are concentrated in construction, domestic and care work, agriculture, hospitality and transport, but this dashboard does not chart that for lack of an authoritative and current source. The border-based long stay estimates still understate informal and short-term movement, so the labour migration totals shown here should be read as lower bounds.

Sources & methodology

The numbers of the Republic of Moldova tell a story that is still unfinished. The diaspora is large, the population is shrinking, remittances keep the economy afloat, and hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians have passed through in search of safety, of whom about 141,000 remain under protection. What comes next will define the country for a generation. The open questions are whether emigrants return, whether the refugee wave recedes, and whether remittances outlast the diaspora. The data below is where that story currently stands.

The story in three lines

  1. About one in four people born in the Republic of Moldova lives abroad. The recorded diaspora is 864,257 people, and Italy has overtaken Russia as the leading destination.
  2. The country hosts about 141,000 Ukrainian refugees, one of the highest counts per resident anywhere in Europe.
  3. Money sent home is worth about 10.5 percent of GDP, roughly double the world average, and since 2022 it comes overwhelmingly from the West.
